# Phase C-8.1 / C-8.2 -- Class-2 Fixture and Composite OCR-Trigger Evaluation

**Doc status:** CLOSED -- decision record. Last reviewed: 2026-08-15. Purpose: preserves why the
composite OCR trigger was rejected (missed a real class-2 failure, plus 3 false-positive
mechanisms) so it isn't silently re-proposed later. The supporting code referenced below
(`CompositeOcrTrigger.java`, `ClassTwoOcrTriggerFixtureTest.java`) has since been deleted
(2026-08-15) -- it was a diagnostic-only fixture/test pair with no further use once this decision
was made; the findings below are the durable record.

Evidence and tests only, per the PM's explicit scope for this work. No production code changed,
no `RoutingTextAcquirer` change, no OCR calls added, nothing committed, nothing wired into a
Spring bean. Builds directly on the prior C-8 investigation notes (`c8-investigation-report.md`,
`c8-trigger-investigation.md`), which identified a gap in the fixture corpus and recommended a
composite trigger without being able to validate it against the one case it matters most for.

## 1. The class-2 fixture

`PdfFixtureBuilder.buildReconciledSummaryNoBalanceColumnSample()` builds a one-page statement:

- A printed summary grid ("Total Debits" / "Total Credits" / "Debit Count" / "Credit Count")
  matching the ledger exactly.
- A ledger with only Date / Narration / Withdrawals / Deposits -- **no balance column at all.**

This is a real gap in the existing corpus: every other running-balance fixture in
`PdfFixtureBuilder` includes a balance column. `closingBalance`/`openingBalance` are populated
*only* from `PdfPreviewGenerator.buildDetectedAccountInfo`'s balance-point derivation (verified by
direct reading -- there is no other path that ever sets them on the PDF import path), and that
derivation requires a `balance`/`running balance`/`closing balance` column on the ledger. Remove
that column and the field is not merely null by omission -- it cannot be populated by any
mechanism that exists today, no matter how clean the rest of the statement is.

## 2. Concrete evidence the native path genuinely misses the field

Ran through the real, unmodified pipeline (`PdfPreviewGenerator` with real
`BalanceChainValidator`/`StatementTotalsValidator`/`SummaryTotalsValidator`, only categorization/
duplicate-detection mocked -- same construction as `ClosingBalanceEvidenceVerticalSliceTest`):

- 3 rows staged, all normalize cleanly.
- `SUMMARY_TOTALS` → **VERIFIED** -- a real, independent, positive corroboration: the parsed
  rows reconcile against the bank's own printed counts/totals, not merely "nothing to check".
- `section.detectedAccount().closingBalance()` and `.openingBalance()` → **both null.**
- `STATEMENT_TOTALS` → `NOT_APPLICABLE` (no balance to check against).
- `BALANCE_CHAIN` → `NOT_APPLICABLE` (no balance column to chain).
- No rule anywhere reports `FAILED` or `WARNING`.

Under every existing native-only signal, this statement looks exactly as clean as a genuinely
complete one. That is the class-2 shape ADR-006 §4b's escalation exists for.

## 3. The composite trigger, evaluated against the class-2 fixture

**Result: the trigger does not fire.** None of its four conditions (no table located / table
located with ~zero rows / `BALANCE_CHAIN` or `STATEMENT_TOTALS` `FAILED` /
`SUMMARY_TOTALS` `PRINTED_ACTIVITY_WITH_ZERO_STAGED`) are about a field being silently absent
while everything else looks fine, and none of them fire here. `CompositeOcrTrigger.shouldEscalate`
returns `false` on the class-2 fixture's signals (`classTwoFixture_compositeTriggerDoesNotFire`).

This is the headline finding of C-8.1: **the composite trigger, as specified in the prior
investigation, does not detect the exact scenario it was built to catch.** It was never designed
to look for a missing field directly -- the prior investigation explicitly flagged "missing
load-bearing field, used naively" as unreliable without product-type context and excluded it from
the recommendation -- but this is the first concrete demonstration that the resulting composite
has a real, non-hypothetical hole, not just a theoretical one.

## 4. Negative cases (C-8.2)

Two of six negative cases behaved as expected. Four surfaced genuine findings -- some in the
direction of "does not over-fire" as hoped, two in the direction of "does over-fire, and here is
exactly why."

| Case | Expected | Actual | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genuinely complete statement, signals read from `section.verification()` directly | no fire | **fires** | Over-fires -- see below |
| Genuinely complete statement, signals recomputed against real transaction rows | no fire | no fire | As expected |
| Single 5.00 discrepancy on the *last* row of an otherwise-perfect 4-row statement | no fire | **fires** | Over-fires -- see below |
| Dormant account, zero transactions, printed summary confirms zero activity | no fire | **fires** | Over-fires -- see below |
| Ordinary per-row parse noise alongside a reconciled statement | no fire | no fire | As expected |
| Sparse-but-legitimate statement (few rows, nothing else wrong) | no fire | no fire | As expected -- confirms the page-ratio heuristic exclusion holds |
| *(positive control)* table located, zero rows, summary claims activity | fire | fires | As expected -- the one condition the prior investigation had strong evidence for |

### Finding A -- balance-marker pseudo-rows poison the trigger on the reference "happy path" fixture

`separate_debit_credit_balance_sample.pdf` (the project's own golden "everything worked" fixture)
stages its `OPENING BALANCE` / `CLOSING BALANCE` marker lines as ordinary transaction rows (each
typed `EXPENSE` with an amount equal to the whole balance). `section.verification()` -- the
findings a naive C-8.3 implementation would most likely read directly -- reports `BALANCE_CHAIN`
as `WARNING` and `STATEMENT_TOTALS` as `FAILED` on this fixture, because both validators see the
marker rows as real activity. **A composite trigger wired directly against
`section.verification()`'s existing findings would have escalated the project's own canonical
correct-import fixture to OCR.**

This is a pre-existing pipeline quirk, not something this task introduced or is authorized to fix
(no production behaviour change was in scope). It was previously un-tested against
`section.verification()`'s raw output -- `ClosingBalanceEvidenceVerticalSliceTest` already works
around it by filtering marker rows (`realTransactionRows`) before calling validators directly, but
nothing previously demonstrated what happens if that filter is skipped, which is exactly what a
first C-8.3 implementation would be at risk of doing if it reused the existing `VerificationReport`
instead of recomputing. Recomputing the same validators directly against filtered real-transaction
rows (mirroring `ClosingBalanceEvidenceVerticalSliceTest`'s own pattern) gives the expected clean
result: `BALANCE_CHAIN` VERIFIED, `STATEMENT_TOTALS` VERIFIED, trigger does not fire.

**Action for C-8.3, if it proceeds:** any composite-trigger implementation must recompute its
input validator findings against real transaction rows (excluding balance-marker pseudo-rows), not
read `StagedAccountSection.verification()` as-is. This should probably also be raised as its own,
separate backlog item regardless of C-8.3's fate, since the marker-row pollution affects the
*existing*, already-shipped `VerificationReport` a user sees today, not just a hypothetical future
trigger -- a `WARNING`/`FAILED` on a correctly-imported statement's own verification panel is a
real user-facing signal, produced by, and currently masked by nobody in the existing test corpus.

### Finding B -- `StatementTotalsValidator`'s zero tolerance defeats `BalanceChainValidator`'s anti-noise guard

Built a fixture (`buildSingleTrailingBalanceDiscrepancySample`) correct everywhere except the last
row's printed balance, off by 5.00 -- a single, scattered, non-cascading discrepancy, placed
deliberately so it cannot propagate into a second one. `BalanceChainValidator`'s own anti-noise
guards work exactly as documented: `WARNING`, not `FAILED` (1 discrepancy of 4 checked pairs, well
under both `MIN_DISCREPANCIES_FOR_FAILED` and `FAILED_THRESHOLD`).

But `StatementTotalsValidator` has **no threshold at all** -- any nonzero difference is `FAILED`,
by design (verified directly: `difference.signum() == 0` is the only branch that is not `FAILED`,
once both balances are present). So the same 5.00 discrepancy independently fails
`STATEMENT_TOTALS`, and because the composite trigger's condition 3 is an OR across both
validators, **the trigger fires anyway** -- `BalanceChainValidator`'s carefully-tuned anti-noise
guard provides no actual protection once it is OR'd with a zero-tolerance validator answering a
closely related question about the same data.

This is not a defect in either validator; each does exactly what its own doc comment says. It is a
property of combining them this way for a *new* purpose (an OCR trigger) that neither was designed
for -- `StatementTotalsValidator`'s zero tolerance is appropriate for "flag this for human review",
much less obviously appropriate for "burn OCR compute on every rounding-level mismatch across the
whole statement corpus."

### Finding C -- condition 2 does not distinguish "dormant" from "broken"

A statement with a located table, zero staged rows, and a printed summary that explicitly
confirms zero debits/credits/counts (a genuinely dormant account, correctly read) still trips
condition 2 ("table located but ~zero rows staged"), because that condition only looks at row
counts -- it never consults what the summary says. `SUMMARY_TOTALS` correctly stays
`NOT_APPLICABLE` here (no false contradiction), but that finding plays no role in condition 2's
own evaluation. The trigger cannot tell a legitimately empty statement, where OCR could not
possibly help, from a table that failed to parse.

(This one case, unlike A and B, was evaluated with hand-built `Signals` rather than a bespoke PDF
fixture -- `tableLocated=true, rows=0` was modelled directly on `DocumentClassification`'s own
existing `LAYOUT_UNSUPPORTED` signal shape, sections ≥ 1 and rows == 0, rather than built as a new
PDF. The other two findings (A, B) are fixture-and-pipeline-verified end to end; this one is a
verified property of the trigger logic given a signal combination the codebase's own
`DocumentClassification` already treats as real and reachable.)

## 5. Verdict

**The composite trigger as specified is insufficient, in two different directions at once, and
needs further work before C-8.3 should build a routing seam around it:**

1. **It misses what it was built for.** The class-2 fixture -- native succeeds, rows reconcile
   against the bank's own printed totals, a load-bearing field is genuinely absent -- does not
   trip any of the trigger's four conditions. None of them reason about field presence at all.
2. **It over-fires in at least three distinct, non-overlapping ways** even before field-presence
   is added: on the project's own reference "correct import" fixture if wired against the existing
   `VerificationReport` (Finding A -- avoidable, but only if the implementer knows to avoid it); on
   a single rounding-level discrepancy, because `StatementTotalsValidator`'s zero tolerance
   overrides `BalanceChainValidator`'s deliberately-tuned guard (Finding B -- not obviously
   avoidable without changing one of the two validators' semantics or excluding
   `StatementTotalsValidator` from the OR); and on legitimately dormant accounts, because condition
   2 does not consult the printed summary at all (Finding C).

None of these are reasons the underlying ADR-006 §4b policy is wrong -- native-first,
evidence-gated escalation is still the right shape. They are reasons this specific four-condition
composite is not yet the right *threshold*, exactly as the ADR itself anticipated when it said the
threshold, not the policy shape, was the open question. Before C-8.3 builds a routing seam around
this trigger, at minimum: (a) decide what "missing load-bearing field" signal condition 2 should
gain, and how to keep it from over-firing the way condition 2 already does without it; (b) decide
whether `StatementTotalsValidator` needs its own tolerance/threshold notion before feeding an OR
gate, or whether the trigger should stop reading it directly; (c) settle on where the trigger's
signals come from (never raw `VerificationReport`, always recomputed against real transaction
rows) as a documented precondition, and treat the marker-row pollution in the existing
`VerificationReport` as its own, separately-worth-fixing item regardless of C-8.3's outcome.
